## Proposals

<!-- proposals:start -->
These are proposals only. **No default has been changed.** Each one cites the measurements above,
and the decision is left to the maintainers.

### P1: float16-specific exponent-lane threshold (recommended)

**Evidence.**
- All 37 float16 false positives (1.29% of float16 tensors) are attention **bias** vectors of 2,304–3,072 elements in three older LLMs: Pythia-160m, Pythia-410m and BLOOM-560m.
- Those 37 tensors score 6.51–6.85, just above the 6.5 threshold, and none has a single out-of-range value.
- Across all float16 tensors, the clean p99 is 6.30. No float16 tensor of 4,096 elements or more scored above 6.22.
- The float16 high byte also holds two mantissa bits, so its entropy runs higher than the float32 sign/exponent byte. Random data measured 7.6–8.0 even at 256 elements.

**Proposal.** Use an exponent-lane threshold of **7.2 for float16** and keep 6.5 for float32 and bfloat16. That clears all 37 false positives with a 0.35-bit margin. Payload sensitivity on float16 holds up, because the out-of-range rule had zero float16 false positives and is what catches partial overwrites.

**Effect.** 3 of the 54 scanned models stop failing at `--fail-on medium`, taking the model-level false-positive rate from 7.4% to 1.9%. Until this is adopted, use `--exponent-threshold 7.2` for float16 checkpoints.

### P2: keep the low-plane floor at 7.5 and document the one DETR hit

**Evidence.** One float32 tensor out of 8,441 tripped the rule: `facebook/detr-resnet-50` · `model.decoder.layers.0.self_attn.v_proj.weight`, with a low plane of 5.94 and a normal exponent lane of 2.42. A trained weight with structured low bits is a genuine anomaly, but this one is benign.

**Proposal.** Do not lower the floor. A floor of 5.5 would clear this tensor, but it would also miss payloads with about 6 bits per byte, such as base64 text, which sit between 5.5 and 7.5. The ASCII test payload scored 4.42. Record this tensor as a known false positive. The treatment `aegis disarm` removes the structure at no measurable accuracy cost; see docs/disarm_impact.md.

### P3: packed quantized tensors need format-aware analysis, not a new threshold

**Evidence.** No rule fired on any packed quantized tensor:

| Dtype | Source | Tensors | Max pooled entropy |
|---|---|---|---|
| int32 | GPTQ/AWQ `qweight` | 2,562 | 7.60 |
| uint8 | bitsandbytes 4-bit | 596 | 7.77 |
| FP8 E4M3 | FP8-dynamic | 168 | 6.88 |

Only the pooled-entropy rule (7.92) applies to integer and FP8 tensors.

**Proposal.** Do not change the default yet, but treat this as a known limit. bitsandbytes 4-bit tensors sit 0.15 bits below the threshold, which leaves two problems:
- Larger models may cross the threshold and produce false positives.
- An encrypted payload in a packed tensor (about 8.0) is hard to tell apart from clean data (about 7.8).

The right fix is a format-aware rule that unpacks the 4-bit values using the quantization config and tests their distribution, together with the FP8 exponent-lane rule that FP8 tensors currently lack. Until then, the scanner offers little assurance about payloads inside packed quantized tensors, and docs/THREAT_MATRIX.md should say so.

### Not measured

- Ten repos had no safetensors at their pinned revision and were skipped.
- Repos over 3 GB were excluded by `--max-gb`, so 7B+ models are not represented. Their larger tensors may behave differently.
- The assumption that these repos are clean is not verified. A true positive among them would be counted as a false positive here.
